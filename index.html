Why Scholia

Answered without putting the book down.

A single unfamiliar word can cost you a chapter. You set the book down, find your phone, and work through a dictionary, a search, and three half-read articles — none of which know which book you're holding, and one of which casually tells you how it ends. By the time you've found your place again, the thread has gone.

Scholia closes that gap entirely. Photograph the page you're on, ask, and carry on — it knows the book, it knows how far you've read, and it stops exactly there.

No spoilers. Ever.

It answers everything except the ending.

This is the part a general chatbot can't do. Search a character's name and the first result tells you how they die. Scholia knows the page you're on, so it can answer in full — and stop exactly there.

Why does Raskolnikov keep going back to the scene?
Because he can't leave it alone — and that's the point Dostoevsky is making about him. Up to where you are, he's built a theory to justify what he did, and his body keeps disagreeing with it.
Does he get caught?
That comes later in the book — I'll keep it unspoiled.

Protection is on by default and set per book. When you do want to look ahead, ask for it — there's a "preview next chapter" button. It just never volunteers.

Q & A

The questions we actually get.

What is Scholia, exactly?

A reading companion for physical books. Photograph the page you're on and ask whatever you like — what a passage means, who a character is, what you should remember before the next chapter. Scholia knows the book, and it knows how far you've read.

Does it work across different editions?

Yes, and it's the part we're proudest of. Scholia never relies on page numbers, because your paperback and someone else's hardback don't agree on them. It reads the actual words on your page and matches them to the book, so any printing or translation works.

Is it really spoiler-free?

Answers are anchored to the page in front of you and to the chapter you've reached. Spoiler protection is deliberate and strict: nothing beyond your position is discussed. It's careful rather than infallible, so we say that plainly — and you can switch it off per book whenever you want the whole thing on the table.

Which books are supported?

Search a title and Scholia tells you honestly how well it knows that specific book. Popular fiction, classics and well-known non-fiction are covered in real depth. For rarer titles it leans on the page you've photographed and says it's doing so, rather than inventing something.

What happens to the photos of my pages?

The text is recognised on your phone, by the phone. The photograph itself is never stored on our servers and never uploaded. What travels is the text of the page, and only to work out where you are and answer your question.

How long does an answer take?

Seconds. Photograph the page, confirm where you are, and the answer arrives before you've settled back into the chair.

What do I actually get for free?

A batch of questions when you install the app — no card, no trial clock. Every feature is unlocked from the first one: scanning, spoiler protection, your whole shelf and reading record. They're enough to use Scholia properly on a real book and decide for yourself, rather than enough to look at a demo. When they run out, Premium is unlimited, monthly or yearly. Everything you asked before that stays readable forever, whether you pay or not.

What's coming next?

Chapter recaps for when you've been away from a book, importing the shelf you already track elsewhere, and a way to look back over everything a book taught you. The core promise doesn't change: your book, your page, no spoilers.
